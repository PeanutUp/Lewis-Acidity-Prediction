Please consider “Coordination Constraints and Information Content in Predicting Lewis Acidity” as a Full Paper for *Digital Discovery*.

Fluoride ion affinity is useful for screening Lewis acids, but the cost of prediction depends on which molecular information must be computed first. Our study uses FIA49k to examine that trade-off across models based on connectivity, optimized acid geometry, and a lower-level calculation of fluoride binding. Matched test molecules, chemical subgroup analysis, and paired structures show where each input helps. Geometry is particularly informative for tridentate ligand environments, while fluoride-adduct calculations enable accurate refinement for difficult P(III) centers. The paired geometry-source experiment identifies a subset for which changing the geometry preparation method materially changes prediction errors.

The study addresses how to build screening workflows that combine machine learning with quantum chemistry. The repository provides the split manifests, prediction tables, configurations, and model checkpoints needed to examine the results and adapt the benchmark.
