{\small\bfseries Abstract}\par
{\small Predicting fluoride ion affinity (FIA) across diverse Lewis acids requires a balance between molecular information and the cost of obtaining it. We evaluate geometric neural networks, molecular descriptors and quantum-chemical correction models on 48,986 FIA49k molecules, including a separate holdout of ligand-scaffold combinations. DimeNet++ gives a main-test mean absolute error of 11.21 $\pm$ 0.27 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ across three training seeds, while a correction to PBEh-3c FIA reduces the error from 23.33 to 3.29 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ and retains an error of 3.35 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ on the scaffold holdout. Matched-ligand comparisons reveal systematic central-element dependence in the reference-method correction, and a frozen-model geometry-source test identifies an additional deployment error, together defining distinct workflows for acid-geometry prediction and adduct-assisted quantum refinement.}\par\vspace{.7cm}
